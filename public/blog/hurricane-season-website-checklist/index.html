Hurricane Season Website Checklist for Lee County Businesses

By Daniel · November 15, 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer

Before hurricane season, make sure at least two people can update your website and Google Business Profile, prepare a short closure notice you can post in seconds, and back up your site. During a storm, mark special hours or a temporary closure on Google and add a banner to your site. Afterwards, post a clear reopening message, update photos and hours, and ask regulars for fresh reviews.

Hurricane season runs from June 1 to November 30. If you've lived in Lee County for a while, you already have a storm routine for your home and your business. Your website and Google listing deserve a spot on that list, because in the days around a storm they're where customers look first.

Before the season starts

  • Know who can make changes. Write down who can log into your website, your domain and your Google Business Profile. Make sure at least two people can, and that the logins aren't on a computer that might be offline.
  • Write your closure notice now. A two-line message you can paste anywhere: when you're closing, how to reach you, and where you'll post updates. It's much easier to write on a calm day in May.
  • Back up your site. If it's hosted on a platform like Cloudflare, your files are already stored away from Florida. If it lives on a small local server, ask whoever runs it where the backups are kept.
  • Check your contact details. A phone line that rings at a flooded office helps nobody. Consider listing a mobile number or a form that reaches you anywhere.

When a storm is coming

  • Update Google first. In your Business Profile you can set special hours for specific dates or mark the business as temporarily closed. Use special hours if you'll reopen soon; Google treats a temporary closure differently from a business that's shut for good.
  • Add a banner to your site. One line at the top of every page with your status and the date you posted it. Dates matter, because an undated "closed" notice still showing in October makes people think you never reopened.
  • Post once, link everywhere. Put your updates in one place, such as your site or your Google posts, and point your social accounts there so information doesn't conflict.

If you're a business people need after a storm

Roofers, tree services, plumbers, electricians, restoration companies and generator techs get flooded with searches after a storm. If that's you, say clearly on your site and Google listing which towns you're covering, whether you're taking new customers, and how fast you can respond. Being honest about a waiting list builds more trust than an unanswered phone.

After you reopen

  • Remove the banner and replace it with a short reopening note for a week or two.
  • Update your hours, and your photos if anything changed.
  • If you moved, update your address everywhere: website, Google, Apple Maps, Yelp and any directories.
  • Ask a few loyal customers for a fresh review. Recent reviews tell new customers you're open and busy.

Let someone else handle it

On a care plan, all of this is one text to me: I post the notice, update Google and put up the banner, then change it all back when you reopen. Either way, a few minutes of preparation now saves a scramble later.
