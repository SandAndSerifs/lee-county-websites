Redesigning Your Website and Keeping Your Google Rankings

By Daniel · November 15, 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer

The way to keep your Google rankings through a redesign is to record which pages and searches bring you visitors before you change anything, keep your best pages at the same addresses where possible, set permanent 301 redirects from every old address to its new one, keep the content that's earning traffic, and watch Search Console closely for a month after launch to fix errors quickly.

A redesign should make your site better at bringing in work, not reset it to zero. The rankings you have now took time to earn. Most of the damage people blame on a redesign comes from a handful of avoidable mistakes. Here's how to avoid them.

1. Take stock before you change anything

Make a list of every page on your current site and how many visitors each one gets. If you have Google Search Console, export the searches each page shows up for. If you don't have it, set it up now; even a few weeks of data helps. Also note any other websites that link to you, like a chamber of commerce or local news story.

2. Keep what's working

If a page brings in steady calls, keep its address and its most useful content. You can improve the design and tighten the wording, but don't throw away the paragraphs Google is ranking it for.

3. Redirect every old address

When a page address changes, set a permanent (301) redirect from the old address to the new one. That tells Google where the page went and passes along most of its value. Without redirects, every old link becomes a dead end, and those pages drop out of search.

4. Don't block search engines by accident

New sites are often built with a setting that hides them from Google during development. Make sure it's turned off at launch. It's one of the most common reasons a freshly redesigned site disappears from search.

5. Watch the first month closely

After launch, check Search Console for crawl errors and pages that aren't indexed, submit your new sitemap, and click through the site on a phone. Some movement in rankings is normal for a few weeks. A steady drop usually points to a missing redirect or a removed page.

6. Make the most of the new site

A redesign is a good moment to add what was missing: pages for each main service, a clear service area naming the Lee County towns you cover, faster images and structured data. Done well, you come out ahead of where you started.
