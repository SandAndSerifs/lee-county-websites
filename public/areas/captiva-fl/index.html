Doing business in Captiva

Captiva is a narrow island at the end of the road, past Sanibel and over the Blind Pass bridge. It's known for sunsets over the Gulf, a small village of restaurants and galleries, and boat trips to islands you can't drive to. Its customers plan trips around a feeling, and your site has to deliver it.

Selling an evening, not just a service

People come to Captiva for sunset dinners, beach weddings, boat trips to Cabbage Key and Cayo Costa, and a slower few days than the mainland offers. They often book from far away and months ahead. A site that shows the experience in real photos, then answers the practical questions (price, dates, what's included, how to get here) is the one that gets the inquiry.

Help visitors with the drive

Getting to Captiva means the Sanibel Causeway and the full length of Sanibel first, so timing matters, especially for anything at sunset. A short "getting here" note with realistic drive times from the causeway, parking details and when to leave keeps guests from showing up late and unhappy.

The island has also been through Hurricane Ian in 2022 and Milton in 2024. Recent photos and a clear line about what's open now reassure people who remember the news.

What that could look like

Consider a wedding photographer who shoots on Captiva's beaches. The site would open with a few of their best sunset ceremonies, then packages with prices or starting prices, the beaches and venues they work at, and an inquiry form that asks for the date and guest count. A planning page covers beach rules, the best months and how to time a ceremony to the sunset.

A Captiva site should have

  • Photos that show the real experience
  • Prices, packages or starting rates
  • Inquiry or booking on the first screen
  • Getting here: drive time, parking, timing
  • A clear line about what's open now
