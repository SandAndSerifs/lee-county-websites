Doing business in Fort Myers Beach

Fort Myers Beach has been rebuilding since Hurricane Ian, and visitors are coming back to a town that looks different from the one they remember. A current website is how they find out you're here and open.

Tell people what's changed

Many businesses on Estero Island reopened in new buildings, temporary spaces or new locations after the 2022 storm. Returning visitors search for the places they remember and often find old information. A site that clearly says where you are now, what you offer and when you're open, backed by a matching Google Business Profile, wins those searches.

Decisions made on the sand

A lot of beach customers decide on the spot, standing on the sand or walking toward Times Square, looking for lunch, a parasail, a bike or a boat. They need prices, hours and a booking button without scrolling. Heavy pages and pop-ups lose them on a weak beach signal, so I keep pages light and fast.

What that could look like

Think of a made-up watersports rental near the pier. The site opens with today's hours and a weather note you can change in seconds, a short price list for jet skis, paddleboards and parasailing, and a booking button. A page covers what to bring, age limits and the cancellation policy for bad weather.

A Fort Myers Beach site should have

  • Where you are now, stated plainly
  • Today's hours and a quick status note
  • Prices and booking without scrolling
  • Light pages for weak beach signal
  • Business Profile updated with your new details
