Doing business in Alva

Alva is the country side of Lee County: a small community on the Caloosahatchee River along State Road 80, with farms, ranches and big lots between Fort Myers and LaBelle. Businesses here are often a family and a few acres, and the website should be just as straightforward.

Getting people down the right road

Rural addresses trip up map apps, and the last mile is often a dirt road or an unmarked gate. A good Alva site gives plain directions from State Road 80 or North River Road, says where to park a truck and trailer, and lists the hours someone is there to open up. That one section prevents a lot of lost visitors and missed sales.

Built for the way people out here live

Alva draws riders and campers to Caloosahatchee Regional Park's equestrian and bike trails, boaters through the W.P. Franklin Lock, and families looking for land, horses and a slower pace. That supports boarding barns, riding lessons, farm stands, feed and tack, fishing guides, well and septic services, fencing and land clearing. Many of those customers are on a phone with weak signal, so I keep pages light, photos compressed and the phone number at the top.

What that could look like

Picture a horse boarding barn off North River Road. The site would show the barn, paddocks and arena in real photos, list boarding options with prices or price ranges, and explain what's included, like feed, turnout and stall cleaning. A short form lets an owner ask about openings and book a visit, and a directions section covers the trailer entrance.

A Alva site should have

  • Directions that work on rural roads
  • Light pages for slow connections
  • Phone number at the top of every page
  • Real photos of your land, animals or work
  • Prices or price ranges and what's included
